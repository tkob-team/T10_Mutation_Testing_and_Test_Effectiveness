## **2\. Reflection Questions**

### **1\. How did AI support your work?**

*Describe specifically where and how AI tools contributed to this project: test design, scenario brainstorming, code generation, log analysis, defect triage, documentation, presentation prep, or other tasks.*

I used Claude for the research notes, tutorial assertions and Cart test workflow. Claude Code supported repository execution, reports and prompt-log tooling. My C01 tutorial runs supplied actual local outputs, while later Agent runs are recorded separately. The assistance connected specification questions with concrete tests and mutant reports rather than only producing prose.

### **2\. What did you accept, reject, or revise from AI outputs?**

*Identify which AI suggestions you incorporated, which you discarded, and how you adapted material to fit the spec, ISTQB principles, or your own analysis.*

I accepted the tutorial assertion code and the five Cart AI assertions. I rejected the explanation that predicted a numeric-constant mutant not generated by Stryker. I corrected the missing SRS interpretation, the assumed date-based workspace and misleading G01 header claims. The log extractor also required revisions for local commands, empty sessions and repeated prompts.

### **3\. What errors, biases, or limitations did you notice in the AI outputs?**

*Concrete examples: hallucinated functions, wrong boundary, missed security case, confused metrics (e.g., p95 vs top 5%), invented citations, etc.*

The main limitation was confident reasoning from incomplete context. Omitting README.md led to incorrect scope questions. Some Cart oracles used implementation-shaped arrays or error fields that the specification did not define exactly. A higher kill count can also reflect a wider set of test scenarios instead of a better generation method.

### **4\. How did you verify information, sources, or claims provided by AI?**

*Describe the verification method (ran the test, checked the spec, looked up RFC, asked TA, cross-checked another tool, executed on real SUT).*

I calculated tutorial expected values, ran Jest and Stryker locally, and compared actual killedBy information. For the later -cc runs, I used the retained execution reports and source references. The comparison keeps Killed and Timeout separate and identifies original defects that a correct test should not hide.

### **5\. What did you learn through this project that you could not have learned by simply accepting AI output?**

*Reflect on your own intellectual contribution. How did AI shape (or fail to shape) your understanding of testing? What is one principle you will keep for future work?*

My recorded comparison identifies the key lesson: an oracle can kill more mutants by depending on current implementation details while becoming less faithful to the business specification. Additional manual scenarios also change the comparison scope. I will keep the requirement, the distinguishing input and the observed state explicit before treating a kill as evidence of test quality. Another documented lesson concerns correcting mutants: a survivor may expose an original defect rather than weak testing. Preserving that defect and its proper oracle is more useful than changing expected results solely to increase the official mutation score.
